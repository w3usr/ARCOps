# ARCOps and space weather

ARCOps (Amateur Radio Club Operations) began as a way for one club to schedule its operators. Its
scheduling tools are the first step of a larger plan: getting operators at many clubs on the air
at short notice when space weather happens, so that their contacts and spots can be used to study
how the ionosphere responds.

## Why

HamSCI (Ham Radio Science Citizen Investigation) is carrying out work funded by NASA under award
No. 80NSSC25K7026. Part of that work is building a community of amateur radio operators who can
get on the air at short notice when a space-weather event happens. The proposal calls this program
**HamSCI QRV** (QRV is the Q-code for "I am ready": on the air). It means short-notice QSO parties
targeting events such as halo coronal mass ejections, corotating interaction regions and meteor
showers. The contacts and spots these operators make, for example on the Reverse Beacon Network,
then serve as measurements of the ionosphere during the event.

## Phase 1: scheduling (in use)

ARCOps schedules a club's operators into time slots, with a roster that knows who may legally and
physically run the station; it runs check-in, and handles station access agreements and license
checks.
At W3USR it has turned contest weekends, such as ARRL Sweepstakes in November 2026, into staffed
operating sessions, where students also learn space weather. The FRC–HamSCI Contesting Dashboard's
real-time space-weather strip, and the HIDRA data assimilation, support that teaching.

Getting operators on the air at a set time is the capability HamSCI QRV needs. What is missing is
the trigger and the reach.

## Phase 2: space-weather alerts (planned)

- **The trigger.** Space-weather alerts that create a QRV event, schedule its slots and notify
  the operators who have asked to hear about it.
- **The reach.** Use by other university clubs, starting with K2MFF (New Jersey Institute of
  Technology) and W8EDU (Case Western Reserve University), and then more widely through HamSCI.

The plan for this phase, from requirements to testing, is set out in the Phase 2 planning issue,
[#23](https://github.com/w3usr/ARCOps/issues/23). Nothing in it is built yet.
